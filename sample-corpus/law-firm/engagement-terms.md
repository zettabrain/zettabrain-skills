# Client Engagement Terms

## Scope of Representation

Sterling & Associates LLP ("the Firm") agrees to provide legal services to the Client as described in the accompanying Engagement Letter. The Firm's representation is limited to the specific matter(s) identified and does not extend to other legal matters unless separately agreed in writing.

## Client Responsibilities

The Client agrees to:
1. Provide complete and accurate information relevant to the matter
2. Respond to communications within 5 business days
3. Pay invoices within the stated terms
4. Inform the Firm of any changes in contact information within 48 hours
5. Not take actions that may prejudice the matter without consulting the Firm

## Communication

- Primary communication via email (encrypted for sensitive matters)
- Status updates provided monthly or upon significant developments
- Client portal access provided within 24 hours of engagement
- Emergency contact available 24/7 for active litigation matters

## File Retention

- Active matter files maintained for duration of engagement
- Closed matter files retained for 7 years
- Original documents returned to Client upon request
- Electronic copies maintained indefinitely at no additional charge
- Client may request file destruction after the 7-year period

## Conflict of Interest

The Firm maintains a conflicts database and performs conflict checks before accepting any new matter. If a conflict arises during representation, the Firm will:
1. Promptly notify affected clients
2. Seek informed written consent where permissible
3. Withdraw from representation if consent cannot be obtained
4. Assist in transitioning to substitute counsel

## Malpractice Insurance

The Firm maintains professional liability insurance with a minimum coverage of $5,000,000 per occurrence and $10,000,000 aggregate. Certificate of insurance available upon request.

## Withdrawal

The Firm may withdraw from representation if:
- The Client fails to pay fees when due (after 30-day notice)
- The Client fails to cooperate or follow reasonable advice
- Continued representation would violate ethical rules
- The Client uses the Firm's services for unlawful purposes

The Firm will provide reasonable notice and assist in transitioning to new counsel.
