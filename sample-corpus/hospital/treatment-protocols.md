# Emergency Department Treatment Protocols

## Triage Classification

### ESI Level 1 - Resuscitation (Immediate)
- Cardiac arrest, respiratory failure, active seizure
- Severe trauma with hemodynamic instability
- Anaphylaxis with airway compromise
- Target: physician contact within 0 minutes

### ESI Level 2 - Emergent (< 10 minutes)
- Chest pain with cardiac history
- Stroke symptoms within 4.5-hour window
- Acute abdomen with vital sign abnormalities
- Altered mental status
- Target: physician contact within 10 minutes

### ESI Level 3 - Urgent (< 30 minutes)
- Abdominal pain, stable vitals
- Fractures (non-displaced, no neurovascular compromise)
- Lacerations requiring sutures
- High fever (>103F) in adults
- Target: physician contact within 30 minutes

### ESI Level 4 - Less Urgent (< 60 minutes)
- Minor injuries, sprains
- Simple UTI symptoms
- Prescription refills (chronic conditions)
- Target: physician contact within 60 minutes

### ESI Level 5 - Non-Urgent (< 120 minutes)
- Minor complaints, rashes
- Suture removal
- Routine wound checks
- Target: physician contact within 120 minutes

## Chest Pain Protocol

1. 12-lead ECG within 10 minutes of arrival
2. Troponin at 0, 3, and 6 hours
3. Aspirin 325mg chewed (if no contraindications)
4. IV access with normal saline at KVO rate
5. Continuous cardiac monitoring
6. Cardiology consult if: ST changes, elevated troponin, HEART score >= 4

## Stroke Protocol (Code Stroke)

1. CT head without contrast within 25 minutes of arrival
2. Blood glucose, CBC, PT/INR, PTT
3. NIH Stroke Scale assessment
4. If ischemic and within 4.5 hours: tPA eligibility assessment
5. Neurology notification within 15 minutes of Code Stroke activation
6. Blood pressure management: permissive hypertension up to 220/120 unless tPA candidate

## Sepsis Protocol (Code Sepsis)

Trigger: qSOFA >= 2 OR suspected infection + SIRS criteria

1. Lactate level immediately
2. Blood cultures x 2 (before antibiotics)
3. Broad-spectrum antibiotics within 1 hour of recognition
4. 30 mL/kg crystalloid bolus for hypotension or lactate >= 4
5. Repeat lactate at 6 hours if initially elevated
6. ICU consultation for vasopressor requirement

## Pediatric Fever Protocol (< 3 months)

All infants under 90 days with temperature >= 100.4F (38C):
1. Full sepsis workup: CBC, blood culture, urinalysis, urine culture
2. Lumbar puncture if < 28 days or ill-appearing
3. Chest X-ray if respiratory symptoms present
4. Empiric antibiotics: Ampicillin + Gentamicin (< 28 days) or Ceftriaxone (29-90 days)
5. Admission for observation minimum 24-48 hours
